About Matthew

I work at the intersection of responsible AI, learning, governance, and human agency.

I am a researcher, strategist, and framework builder focused on how emerging technologies shape judgment, participation, accountability, and public value. My work connects conceptual research with practical frameworks, learning experiences, and responsible innovation strategy.

Rather than treating AI only as a tool, I examine how AI systems enter human contexts: how they shape interpretation, confidence, options, authority, responsibility, and the conditions under which people remain active participants in their own decisions.

What drives the work

The central concern is not only what AI produces, but what it reorganizes.

The central concern behind my work is that AI systems do not only produce outputs. They can influence what people notice, how situations are framed, which possibilities feel available, and where responsibility appears to belong.

That matters because many of the most important effects of AI are not only technical. They are educational, relational, institutional, and civic. They appear in how learners develop judgment, how young people prepare for changing futures, how organizations assign accountability, how communities understand risk, and how people remain capable of questioning, revising, and authoring their own participation.

My work starts from a simple premise: responsible innovation should strengthen human agency, not quietly reorganize it around systems people do not fully understand, contest, or govern.

Building toward

A body of work for wiser participation in AI-mediated life.

I am building toward a body of work that helps people, institutions, and communities participate more wisely in AI-mediated life.

That includes relational AI research, responsible innovation strategy, learning experiences for judgment and agency, youth-centered readiness work, and practical frameworks for examining legitimacy, accountability, and human participation before AI systems become normalized.

The longer-term aim is not only to critique emerging technologies, but to help create the conditions under which they can be questioned, governed, repaired, redirected, and used in ways that preserve human dignity and public value.
